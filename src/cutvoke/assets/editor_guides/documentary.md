# Documentary, travel and observational edits

Layer 2. Use for a factual journey, documentary, event or place-led vlog. For an interview-led argument also read the relevant explanation/interview guidance, not every other workflow.

Build a traceable sequence of arrival, encounter, change, consequence or reflection from the available sources. Keep chronology and causal relationships accurate where they matter. Clearly distinguish illustrative imagery or a nonchronological montage from evidence of a particular event. Retain source IDs, intervals and uncertainty in the selects.

Choose location sound, reactions, pauses and small actions alongside establishing images. Let B-roll form a visual progression under speech: an action starting, developing and completing, a detail revealing its context, or a useful contrast. A library of interchangeable scenery may establish atmosphere but cannot by itself explain an experience.

Test sound bridges where they carry place or anticipation: an incoming location heard before the cut, or an outgoing voice continuing over a reaction. Confirm live audio/clip schemas before separating or shifting picture and sound. Keep source ambience available under music when it is part of the brief; avoid flattening all locations into the same music bed.

Shape pace around observation and meaningful changes. Accelerated montages, elaborate transitions or long holds can all work when motivated by the requested experience. Do not equate factual work with a mandatory subdued style, or travel with repeated drone shots and speed ramps.

Review factual meaning, natural sound handoffs, spatial orientation, reactions and emotional breathing room in motion. A useful test is whether the viewer can describe what was experienced or learned, with visible source support. If timing changes could imply an event that was not recorded, explain that uncertainty and choose a faithful alternative.
