# Music video and artistic visual work

Layer 2. Use for a music-led MV, visual poem, performance montage or a brief explicitly asking for strong artistic expression. Combine another workflow only when its requirements matter, such as factual interview material in an MV.

## Design an arc the viewer can perceive

Identify song sections, changes of instrumentation/energy, vocal entrances, meaningful pauses and selected accents from actual audio or supplied timecodes. Mark uncertainty if listening is unavailable. Select moments for emphasis; cutting on every detected beat can erase contrast. Some music-led projects start from sound, others from a picture assembly: choose according to the brief.

Choose recurring shapes, gestures, motion directions, colors or sounds from the available footage. Decide how the motif changes: introduction, interruption, escalation, resolution. Distinguish performance-led, narrative and abstract passages. A scenic sequence needs more than a list of attractive locations when the brief asks for a visual transformation.

For a signature passage record:

- Viewer sees what at the beginning, what happens, and what remains at the end.
- The action, lyric idea, sound change or musical accent motivating it.
- Source intervals and intended foreground/background relationship.
- How a later use varies the motif, rather than merely repeating the preset.

For example, a circular water motion may reveal a similarly shaped architectural opening, or a sleeve movement may carry a spatial handoff. These are **design intentions**, not claims of supported automatic recognition or one-command effects. Inspect actual shots and available mask, motion, transition and compositing schemas before choosing the implementation.

## Make integration and impact coexist

Plan position, direction, occlusion and the effect's entry/change/exit. Match color, sharpness and existing texture where relevant; preserve the subject's readable action. A powerful treatment can still obey the scene's lighting and depth. Conversely, a subtle treatment is appropriate only when it serves the requested result.

Prototype a representative dynamic passage, often roughly 8–15 seconds, before applying a treatment widely. Compare untreated/simple-cut and designed versions at the same time range and intended viewing size, with music. Inspect onset, fastest motion, subject crossings, handoff and resolution. If the viewer cannot identify the intended addition, rethink timing, composition or the relationship between shots; increasing brightness alone may not solve it.

## Acceptance

Name the passages that deliver the intended expression and point to actual preview/export evidence. Check that repeated choruses develop or deliberately reprise the idea, quieter sections create useful contrast, and effects land on meaningful events. Keep source performance credit distinct from added edit/composite work. A quiet or opaque result can satisfy a quiet brief; a forceful brief needs a perceptible, justified result.
