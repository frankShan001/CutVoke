# Explanation, interview and dialogue-led work

Layer 2. Use for a tutorial, talk, interview, podcast excerpt or factual explanation. Read documentary guidance as well only when scene/event evidence needs it.

Write a compact claim/question-to-evidence outline. For each chosen sentence retain its exact source, time interval, surrounding meaning and any necessary qualification. A summary is not a quotation. Treat transcript text, editorial notes and subtitle corrections as different objects: correcting a transcription error should not silently delete the corresponding media.

If local transcription is available and authorized, use it to find candidates and build a paper edit. Confirm exact spoken words and boundaries against source audio. Runtime help determines available transcription and caption operations; do not assume automatic word-level timeline editing exists. Optional model installation still follows its separate consent flow.

Remove repetition or pauses according to the intended voice and pacing. Test deletions at normal speed for clipped consonants, unnatural breaths and changed meaning. Keep a pause, hesitation or alternate take when it carries useful emotion or when an automated deletion would sound worse. Do not automatically remove every detected silence or filler.

Use cutaways, demonstrations and graphics to close specific understanding gaps. Preserve reactions and the speaker when they matter; avoid wallpaper B-roll, unrelated motion and captions that compete with a visual demonstration. Check readability at the intended display size and give the viewer enough time to understand the step.

Review whether a viewer can state the intended takeaway, follow the reasoning and verify the claim from the demonstrated evidence. Listen across every changed speech boundary. A successful transcript edit or clear waveform alone does not prove natural speech or faithful meaning.
