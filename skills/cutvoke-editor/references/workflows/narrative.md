# Narrative and character-led scenes

Layer 2. Use for fiction, a character scene, or a montage whose main job is to tell an evolving event. For a music-led sequence, combine the music-video reference only when its musical decisions are relevant.

Identify what a character wants, what the viewer knows, the obstacle and the moment something changes. Select performances, looks, reactions and actions with source intervals. Arrange cause, response and payoff; a good reaction may carry more meaning than an additional establishing shot.

Check screen direction, eyelines, action phase, position, time and sound across adjacent shots. Break continuity deliberately when the brief benefits from disorientation, and make the resulting information readable. Try matching action or carrying dialogue/ambience across the cut when it helps anticipation or a reaction. Read current audio and transition schemas before implementing split edits.

Shape the sequence's tension with duration, withheld information and contrast. Where useful, compare a picture/location-sound version with the scored version to find where music masks weak action progression. A music-driven brief may require the opposite working order; muting music is a diagnostic option, not a universal creative rule.

Treat visual effects as events in the scene: a reveal, a changing relationship, a point-of-view shift or a transformation with consequences. For composites, plan foreground order, subject crossings and lighting/motion consistency, then test a short moving sample. An unsupported operation remains a proposal until discovered and rendered.

Review whether the viewer can follow who acts, why the next cut occurs and what has changed. Check both technical continuity and the dramatic effect of a deliberate discontinuity. Preserve editable source performances and avoid redesigning an approved scene during a minor revision.

Practitioner context: [Zack Arnold on montage structure, musical detail and testing without score](https://blog.frame.io/2021/06/02/art-of-the-cut-cobra-kai-zack-arnold/). This is one editor's working method, not a mandatory order for all scenes.
